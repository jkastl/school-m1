For grown-ups

What this is

A digraph is two letters that spell one sound. In sh, you don't hear an /s/ and then an /h/. The two letters together make a brand new sound, /sh/. In class it's tied to the “be quiet” sound, so a finger to the lips is a great reminder.

How to help

  • Stretch the sound: “shhhhh-ip”, “fi-shhhhh.” Ask: is the quiet sound at the start or the end?
  • Play “I spy something with sh” around the house (shoe, brush, dish, shelf, shirt).
  • Write sh words with a finger on the table, in sand, or on a steamy mirror, saying the sound as you write the two letters.
  • When reading, if your child says /s/ /h/ for sh, cover the rest of the word and ask, “What do these two say together?”

Common mix-ups

  • s or sh? sip and ship, sell and shell. A snake (sss) versus a quiet sign (shhh).
  • h and s apart: in a word like house, the h and the s are both there but not side by side, so there's no /sh/.
  • Other digraphs (ch, th, wh, ck) usually come along soon and follow the same idea.
