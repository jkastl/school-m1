Long and short vowels: i and u

Does the vowel say its name?

Learn

a e i o u are vowels. Every vowel can make two sounds: a short sound and a long sound.

Long ī

A long vowel says its name. Long i says “eye.” Long u says “you.”

Short ĭ

A short vowel makes its own sound, like the start of itch or up.

A flat line ( ¯ ) on top means long. A little smile ( ˘ ) means short.

For grown-ups

What this is

Each vowel has a short sound and a long sound. The long sound is the letter's name: /ā/, /ē/, /ī/, /ō/ and /yōō/ for u (as in unicorn). The short sounds are anchored to keywords: /ă/ apple, /ĕ/ edge, /ĭ/ itch, /ŏ/ octopus, /ŭ/ up. The class has been reviewing i and u.

Marks: a macron ( ¯ ) means long and a breve ( ˘ ) means short.

About this practice

  • It's about hearing the difference, not spelling it. Long vowels get spelled several ways (kite, pie, tiger), and patterns like silent e usually come later. If a word is too hard to read, the speaker button reads it.
  • Long u has two sounds in English: /yōō/ as in cube and /ōō/ as in flute. The class card uses /yōō/, so the practice sticks to that one.
  • Kids learn an action for each vowel sound in class. The page doesn't show them, so ask your child to teach you!

How to help

  • Stretch the vowel: “p-iii-g” versus “k-iii-te.” Ask, “Does it say its name?”
  • Sort snacks, toys or groceries by vowel sound.
  • Play I spy: “I spy something with a short u” (cup, rug, bug).
