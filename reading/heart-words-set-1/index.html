In the real world

Heart words are some of the words we use most. Look for them everywhere!

  • Book huntPick a page in a favorite book. How many times can you find is or was?
  • Write a note“That is for you!” has three heart words in it. Draw the hearts!
  • SignsOn a walk or at the store, look for in, on, is and for on signs.
  • Talking“Where are my shoes?” “That is his cup.” We say heart words all day long.
  • Write it bigWrite a heart word in sand, salt or shaving cream. Draw a heart over the tricky part.
  • At the tableTake turns: one person says a heart word, the other spells it out loud.
For grown-ups

What this is

Heart words are high-frequency words that aren't fully decodable yet. Kids sound out the parts that follow the patterns they know and learn the irregular part “by heart.” In class, a heart goes over the tricky letters.

The tricky parts in this set

  • is, his: the s says /z/.
  • was: the a says /ŭ/ and the s says /z/.
  • he: the e says its name, /ē/.
  • for: or says /or/.
  • that: th is a digraph that hasn't been taught yet.
  • are: the whole word is the heart part (a-r-e says /ar/).
  • in, it, on: no heart part. These are fully decodable and just get practiced a lot.

Some heart parts are only tricky for now. Once th, or and long vowels are taught, that, for and he become regular. The a in was stays tricky.

How to help

  • Say, tap, spell, write. Say the word, tap a finger for each sound, spell it out loud, then write it while saying the letter names.
  • Draw a little heart over the tricky part when your child writes the word.
  • A few minutes often beats one long session. Mix in words your child already knows so it feels like a win.
  • If your child guesses from the first letter (his for he), point to each letter and read it together slowly.
